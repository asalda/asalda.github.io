About Me

Hi, I am Alberto! I am an astrophysicist trying to answer one of the big open questions in cosmology: which sources reionized the Universe, and how did their ionizing light escape from galaxies during the first billion years after the Big Bang?

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Science Mission Office at the Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI, Baltimore, USA), working with Dr. Alaina Henry, Dr. Bethan James and Dr. Travis Fischer. Before that, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow in Prof. Matthew Hayes' group at the Astronomy Department of Stockholm University (SU, Sweden).

Research in a nutshell

Massive stars and accreting black holes produce enormous amounts of ionizing radiation, but most of it never leaves its host galaxy: it is absorbed by the gas and dust of the interstellar and circumgalactic media. I follow these photons along their whole journey, from the sources that produce them to the gaseous halos where stellar feedback deposits its energy, in order to understand what regulates their escape and, ultimately, the Reionization of the Universe. To do so, I combine far-ultraviolet to infrared imaging and spectroscopy from space (HST, JWST) and the ground (Keck, VLT), using nearby starburst galaxies as laboratories for their distant counterparts at Cosmic Dawn. Research

My research combines space- and ground-based spectroscopy and imaging of star-forming galaxies, from our cosmic neighborhood to the first billion years, to understand how stellar feedback and ionizing radiation shape galaxies and their surroundings. To date, I have authored 69 papers, including 6 as first author, which together have gathered ~3,000 citations (h-index of 30). I advocate for an ethical and transparent scientific methodology, where discoveries, data and tools are visible and publicly available.

Below are the highlights of my first-author work, from newest to oldest. The complete list can be found on NASA/ADS and in my publication list.

Witnessing the birth of galactic winds

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2026b) · ApJ, 1011, 220

Galactic winds are usually observed long after they are launched, once stellar winds, radiation pressure and supernovae have all contributed to them. In this paper, we used the host galaxies of superluminous supernovae (SLSNe) as a cosmic clock: their very massive progenitors explode only a few million years after a starburst begins, and their bright light reveals the gas in front of them. With VLT/X-shooter spectroscopy of seven hosts, we found slow, low-ionization outflows that can be powered entirely by stellar winds and radiation pressure, before ordinary supernovae kick in. This provides one of the first empirical constraints on the earliest phases of stellar feedback.

The LaCOS survey: Lyman-alpha halos and the escape of ionizing light

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2026a) · ApJ, 999, 71

The circumgalactic medium (CGM) is a vast gas reservoir surrounding galaxies, whose kinematics and composition are linked to the properties of the galaxies themselves. In this paper, we used HST imaging from the Lyα and Continuum Origins Survey (LaCOS) to map the Lyman-alpha halos of nearby star-forming galaxies. We then explored how the morphology and extent of these halos, which trace the CGM in emission, relate to the escape of ionizing radiation.

A JWST view of feedback in high-redshift galaxies

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2025) · MNRAS, 544, 132

Stellar winds and supernovae inject energy and momentum into the interstellar medium (ISM) of galaxies, either triggering or preventing future star formation. This stellar feedback manifests as galactic winds, which remain poorly constrained in the distant Universe. In this paper, we used high-resolution JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy to measure the outflows and dynamical masses of high-redshift galaxies, and tested whether their winds are strong enough to escape their hosts. This work was featured in AstroBites.

  • Read the paper

The VANDELS survey: ionizing properties of galaxies at 3 ≤ z ≤ 5

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2023) · MNRAS, 522, 6295

VANDELS is one of the last ESO public spectroscopic surveys, targeting hundreds of star-forming galaxies in the early Universe. In this paper, we used its deep rest-frame ultraviolet spectra to study the gas geometry and dust content of galaxies at 3 ≤ z ≤ 5, through absorption features imprinted on the light of their stars (the down-the-barrel technique), and to infer their ionizing properties. This paper also presented our Python code FiCUS.

  • Read the paper

The HST Low-Redshift Lyman Continuum Survey (LzLCS)

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2022) · A&A, 663, A59

The LzLCS is the most comprehensive survey of the ionizing output of low-redshift galaxies: at the time of publication, it tripled the number of Lyman continuum (LyC) detections in the nearby Universe. In this paper, we developed the routines to measure the LyC escape fraction (fesc) of these galaxies, and established that neutral gas and dust contribute equally to the absorption of their ionizing radiation. The LzLCS has since become a reference survey for the Reionization community, with many studies making use of our public high-level data products.

  • Read the paper

The Extragalactic Background Light

Saldana-Lopez et al. (2021) · MNRAS, 507, 5144

Galaxies emit light over a wide energy range, from ultraviolet (UV) photons produced by the most massive stars to infrared (IR) radiation re-emitted by the dust that absorbs them. Accumulated over cosmic history, this light builds up the Extragalactic Background Light (EBL). In my first paper, we used the exquisite multiwavelength photometry of the HST/CANDELS fields to determine the spectral intensity of the EBL and its evolution with cosmic time, with implications for gamma-ray astronomy in the era of Fermi and CTA.

Coding

I support open-source and collaborative programming. I believe that the tools behind scientific results should be as accessible as the results themselves, so I make my codes public, documented and easy to install. My main contribution is FiCUS, a Python package to infer the stellar populations of galaxies from their ultraviolet spectra, now used by the community in 10 independent papers.

FiCUS

Fitting the stellar Continuum of Uv Spectra

FiCUS fits the stellar continuum of far-ultraviolet galaxy spectra with stellar population synthesis models and dust attenuation, delivering the key properties of the young stars that power each galaxy:

  • Stellar ages
  • Metallicities
  • UV dust attenuation
  • Ionizing photon production

It was designed for the HST/COS spectra of the Low-Redshift Lyman Continuum Survey (Saldana-Lopez et al. 2022) and applied to deep rest-frame UV spectra at 3 ≤ z ≤ 5 (Saldana-Lopez et al. 2023). The code is publicly available and fully documented on GitHub.
